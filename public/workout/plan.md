# Two-Month Integrated Training Plan
*Elbow rehab · chest development · RP resistance work · climbing · mobility*

---

## How this plan is built

Five source plans, one body, one elbow that's currently the limiting factor. The elbow rehab plan is non-negotiable and runs daily for the full two months regardless of everything else. Everything else is sequenced around it.

**Why two months, not one:** the RP resistance work (curls, rows, deadlifts) and full chest variety are grip- and wrist-extension-heavy — exactly the loading pattern that aggravates lateral epicondylitis. Stacking that onto active rehab risks a flare before the tendon's had a chance to adapt. So:

- **Month 1 (Weeks 1–4):** Elbow rehab + climbing + mobility + *light* chest only. No RP lifting.
- **Month 2 (Weeks 5–8):** Add RP resistance work at full volume. Everything runs as each source doc wrote it, monitored against the flare protocol.

**On the RP 3-Day Table vs. RP Gym-Free doc:** these share the same underlying logic (dumbbell-based, start at 5lb, push to failure, adjust weight if you clear 30 reps or can't hit 5) and several exercises overlap (sumo deadlifts, goblet squats, one-arm rows). But they aren't identical — the Gym-Free image only shows Day 1, and its Day 1 has 10 exercises across 5 combos, while the Table's Day 1 has 6 exercises across two lift groups. I've used the **Table as the backbone** for Month 2 because it's the only source with all three training days fully specified. Where the Gym-Free version diverges (pushups instead of a warm-up-only slot, wall touch raises, reaching situps), I've noted it as a no-equipment substitution option rather than merging two programs that don't actually match exercise-for-exercise.

## Daily non-negotiables (every day, both months)

**Morning (~5 min):**
- Isometric wrist extension hold, light resistance — 45 sec × 3
- Wrist extensor stretch — 30 sec × 3

**Evening (~10 min)** — *skip this block entirely on climbing days:*
- Wrist circles + quick stretch warm-up
- Eccentrics (pick one: dumbbell wrist extension, FlexBar/Tyler Twist, or no-equipment version) — 3 × 15, 4–5 sec lowers, 1 min rest
- Wrist extensor stretch — 30 sec × 2
- Ice only if aching

**Rule of thumb throughout:** mild discomfort (≤3/10) during exercise is fine. Pain lasting 24+ hours means the prior day was too much — cut volume, don't just push through. Sharp pain means reduce load immediately.

**Climbing days:** 15 min easy warm-up climbing before anything hard, open-hand grip only, no crimps/small edges/pockets, stop one climb early — next-morning pain is the real signal.

## Month 1 (Weeks 1–4): rehab-first

**Chest — light only:**
- Incline pushups (hands on desk) only — this is the easier variant and reduces load through the wrist. Hold off on standard, decline, wide-grip, and close-grip pushups, floor press, and floor flys until Month 2; all of these add load precisely where the tendon is healing.
- 2–3 short sessions/day, near-failure but not to failure.
- Doorway chest stretch and cross-body shoulder stretch after each session — these double as elbow-friendly tendon care and cost nothing extra.
- No dedicated pulling work yet (bands/rows) unless you have a band on hand — if you do, 1–2 light sets per pushup session per the chest doc's pressing:pulling ratio.

**RP Lifting:** none. This is the deliberate gap.

**Mobility — Weeks 1–4 of its own 12-week arc:**
- Weeks 1–3: all 8 foundation moves (Child's Pose, 90/90 Hip Switch, Standing Forward Fold, Cat-Cow, Thread the Needle, Doorway Chest Stretch, Calf Stretch, Neck Half-Circles), 30–45s holds, 4–5x/week.
- Week 4: add the Wk4+ moves (Low Lunge/Hip Flexor, Seated Forward Fold, Extended Puppy Pose), deepen holds to 45–60s. Run hip-flexor and hamstring stretches twice per session — they're the slowest to open.

**Goal for Month 1:** consistent daily elbow loading, unbroken climbing rhythm, chest volume held low enough that it's not competing with tendon recovery. Expect 8–12 weeks total before the tendon meaningfully turns a corner — Month 1 is the first half of that runway, not the whole thing.

## Month 2 (Weeks 5–8): full load, monitored

**RP resistance work (Table), 3x/week:**

*Day 1* — Lift A (each to failure, 30 sec rest between): Goblet Squats → Stiff-Legged Deadlifts → Sumo Deadlifts. Lift B (straight through, no rest): Alternating Curls → One-Arm Rows → Crunches with Top Hold. Optional warm-up: Medium Pushups / Chair Press / Upright Rows, 3× to failure.

*Day 2* — Lift A: Sumo Squats (Shoulder Rack) → Sumo Stiff-Legged Deadlifts. Lift B: One-Dumbbell Two-Arm Standing Extensions → Narrow Pushups → Slow-Down Reaching Situps. Optional warm-up: High Bent Rows / Supinating Curls / Paused Side Raises.

*Day 3* — Lift A: Elbows-Out Bent Rows → One-Dumbbell Two-Arm Standing Extensions. Lift B: Hammer Curls → Forward In-Place Lunges → Slow-Down Reaching Situps. Optional warm-up: Bent Upright Rows / Sumo Squats (Shoulder Rack) / Good-Mornings.

Progression rule (both source docs agree): start at 5lb, if you clear 30+ reps on any lift, go up next week; if you can't hit 5 reps, go down.

**No-equipment substitution (from the Gym-Free doc):** if you're without a gym, Day 1 becomes 5 combos done back-to-back (Sumo Deadlifts+Pushups, Goblet Squats+One-Arm Rows, Sumo Good Mornings+Upright Rows, Standing Shoulder Press+Hammer Curls, Wall Touch Raises+Reaching Situps), with combo count scaling 1x→5x→1x across weeks 1–6 of that specific progression. This is a genuinely different volume structure from the Table — pick one and stay with it for Month 2 rather than switching mid-stream.

**Elbow-risk flags for Month 2:** curls, rows (all variants), and stiff-legged/sumo deadlifts are the highest-grip, highest-wrist-extensor-demand movements in this program — they're the ones most likely to trigger a flare. Watch these specifically against the 24-hour pain rule. Extensions and lunges are lower-risk for the elbow.

**Chest — full variety now:**
- All pushup variants (standard, incline, decline, wide/close-grip), 2–3 sessions/day between meetings.
- Floor press and floor flys, 1–2x/week for hypertrophy.
- Pulling work: 1–2 sets for every 2–3 sets of pressing (banded rows, towel rows, reverse flys).
- Stretch after, not before, pressing work (arm circles/band pull-aparts/cat-cow beforehand instead — cold static stretching reduces strength output short-term).
- 48 hrs between chest-loaded sessions of matching intensity; light between-meeting sets short of failure don't need this gap.

**Mobility — Weeks 5–8 of its own arc:**
- Weeks 5–6 (finishing Depth phase): Wk4+ moves at 45–60s holds.
- Weeks 7–8 (starting Bridging to Yoga Shapes): add Half Pigeon, Cobra, Seated Spinal Twist, Downward Dog. Go slow into Half Pigeon. Sessions run 20–25 min now.
- Note: Downward Dog and any extended weight-bearing on flat hands puts the wrist in extension — if the elbow is flaring that week, substitute a forearm-supported variant or skip it rather than pushing through.

## If a flare happens (either month)

1. 3–7 days off climbing — not off everything; keep morning isometrics.
2. Ice 10–15 min after aggravating activity.
3. Meds per Dr. Conley: acetaminophen 1000mg 3×/day and/or ibuprofen 600mg 3×/day **or** naproxen 440–500mg 2×/day — never ibuprofen + naproxen together.
4. Counterforce strap for daily tasks.
5. Drop back to eccentrics-and-isometrics only for the affected movement pattern (e.g., pause curls/rows specifically, keep everything else) until pain settles, then rebuild.

If three months of honest consistency on the elbow doesn't move the needle, that's the trigger to raise shockwave therapy or other options at a doctor visit — worth tracking whether Month 1 + Month 2 combined gets you there or just partway.
